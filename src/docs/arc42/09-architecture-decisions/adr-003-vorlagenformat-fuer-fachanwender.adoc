== ADR-003: Template format and maintenance by business users

=== Status

Proposed.

This ADR was created with AI assistance. It counts as an architecture decision
only once the responsible owner has reviewed and accepted it.

=== Decision

We propose (subject to a prototype, see Follow-Up):

* The **internal template format** for letterheads and letter templates is
  **semantic HTML with CSS Paged Media** (`@page`, A4, DIN 5008 dimensions,
  running headers and footers, page counters).
* **Maintenance by business users** takes place in a **building-block-based
  editor** in the UI:
** Letterhead: a form for type (private, business Form A or B), logo, slogan,
   sender, footer, information block, font, font sizes and spacing.
   Positions according to DIN 5008 are fixed and cannot be moved freely.
** **Style specifications** of the letterhead (named styles such as body text,
   subject, sender line with font, size, line spacing, spacing and colour) are
   transferred once from the central DOCX specification and apply to all
   letter templates that use this letterhead. In the text, business users only
   choose named styles, no free formatting.
** Letter template: a rich text editor (e.g. based on ProseMirror/TipTap)
   that inserts placeholders, repetition blocks and condition blocks as
   building blocks.
* **DOCX** stays the design specification of the business users. A **DOCX
  import** for text content (not for the layout) is possible as a later
  extension.

Option 4 (DOCX upload with conversion) can be combined with option 1 as an
entry path. Whether the conversion works without rework is open and is assessed
in the prototype as well.

=== Context

The owner requires: maintenance by non-technical people, preview and final
document look the same, no commercial tools, PDF/A and accessibility. Today the
business users work with DOCX files, which also hold the design specifications
(font, size, spacing, logo position). A letterhead is a template that a letter
template uses, and it shall be just as maintainable.

Addition by the owner (2026-11-14): The DOCX files come as a specification from
a central department. An editor instead of Word is fine; transferring the parts
into the editor is no problem. In the result, the styles of the specification
must be right (font, font sizes, spacing, positions).

These goals are in tension: DOCX is the most familiar to business users, but
converting DOCX to PDF/A plus accessible HTML is lossy without commercial tools,
and an HTML preview of a DOCX does not exactly match the PDF.

=== Decision Drivers

* Maintainability by business users without developers.
* Identical look of preview and PDF.
* Accessibility (structured content, tagged PDF).
* Only non-commercial open source tools, MIT-compatible.
* Closeness to today's way of working with DOCX.
* A reliable result without invisible rework.

=== Considered Options

==== Option 1: Building-block-based editor, internally HTML and Paged Media CSS

Business users maintain templates in a structured way in the UI. The internal
format is the same for preview and PDF and carries semantics (headings, lists,
alternative texts). Drawback: a new user interface instead of Word; design
freedom is deliberately limited.

==== Option 2: DOCX templates with placeholders, conversion at runtime

Business users stay in Word. Runtime: DOCX templating (e.g. poi-tl,
docx-stamper) and conversion to PDF, in good quality practically only with
headless LibreOffice. The HTML preview is created separately and differs, or
the preview shows the PDF. Heavy runtime dependency (LibreOffice) in the host.

==== Option 3: XSL-FO templates with Apache FOP

Mature PDF/A and tagged PDF generation. Not maintainable by business users; the
HTML preview would need a second transformation.

==== Option 4: DOCX upload with automatic conversion into HTML templates

Business users upload DOCX; the application converts it into the internal HTML
format (e.g. with mammoth). A familiar entry point, but layout fidelity
(positions, spacing) is lost, and rework becomes invisible.

=== Consequences

==== Positive

* A single internal format for preview and PDF.
* Structured content as the basis for accessibility and DIN 5008 dimensional
  accuracy.

==== Negative

* An editor must be built; it is a substantial part of the MVP effort.
* Business users must get used to a new user interface (R-001); the owner considers this acceptable.
* Changes to the central DOCX specification must be carried over manually (R-009).

==== Neutral or Follow-Up

* Prototype (spike) with real DOCX templates from the owner (to be supplied later): rebuild in the editor model, measurement of style fidelity (QS-013), comparison with option 2.
* The result decides between option 1 and option 2; only then can the ADR be accepted.


=== Result of the prototype

The throwaway prototype from #3 (`prototyp/README.adoc`) checked option 1
against `Geschaeftsbrief.docx`, single-page and multi-page, DIN 5008 Form B.

* *Rebuild in the template model:* letterhead and letter template as HTML with
  Paged Media CSS. The dimensions come from the DOCX (page margins, frames,
  tab stops, paragraph styles). Where Word applies its own rules, for example
  for line spacing, they are matched against the baselines of the Word PDF.
* *Distance to the Word PDF:* at most 0.1 mm, same line and page breaks. The
  only difference: in the single-page specification, Word sets an empty second
  page, the prototype does not.
* *Semantics:* The subject is a heading and looks as in the specification
  (owner, 2026-11-20).
* *Template content with findings:* Maintenance shows the findings of the
  sanitization and requires a choice: Ablehnen, Trotzdem akzeptieren or
  Bereinigen (owner,
  2026-11-20, xref:adr-014-isolation-der-vorschau-im-iframe[ADR-014]).
  Implemented in #8 and #9.

Not checked:

* Style fidelity from the point of view of the central department (QS-013). It
  decides on the acceptance of this ADR.
* The editor itself and thus the maintainability by business users.
* Option 4 (DOCX upload with conversion) and the comparison with option 2.
* `Formular_intern.docx`.

=== Assumptions

* Today's DOCX templates use no free-form layout that goes beyond the DIN 5008 building blocks.
* Business users accept guided maintenance if the result matches the print exactly.

=== Open Questions

* Sample templates for the prototype (Q-PO-004): answered, in `src/examples/`.
* How often does the central department change its DOCX specification, and who carries over changes? (Q-PO-009)
